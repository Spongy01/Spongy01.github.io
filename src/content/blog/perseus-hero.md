I was reading about constellations some time back and Perseus stood out. It comes with a really cool backstory (which a lot of Greek constellations share, ig?), so here it is.

## Wait, who is his dad?

Perseus was the son of Zeus (yeah, you find this guy literally everywhere) and a mortal princess named Danae.

## Backstory

But we need to go back a bit. Danae's father, King Acrisius of Argos, got a prophecy long before Perseus was born: his grandson would one day kill him. His solution was to lock Danae away in a bronze chamber so that no one could ever reach her. (Rapunzel, is that you?)

But as fate would have it (and Zeus's mood), Zeus turned into golden rain, slipped in, and Danae ended up with a child. Perseus was born, the secret came out, and Acrisius had a problem. He didn't want to kill his own daughter and grandson directly (the gods tend to frown on that), so he locked them in a wooden chest and threw it into the sea. Technically not murder, which is a very cursed loophole.

## The island and the feast

But Perseus is the hero of the story, so ofc he doesn't die. The chest washed up on the island of Seriphos, where a fisherman took them in. The island's king was Polydectes, and Polydectes liked Danae. A lot.

> Wait, was she really that beautiful? I looked it up. Yes, apparently she was famous for it.

The problem was mama's boy Perseus, who was not going anywhere without his mom. So Polydectes made a plan, and here it is, hahahahahahaha (the evil laugh, if you didn't get it).

He threw a big feast and asked every guest to bring him a horse as a gift. Perseus had no horse, so he said he'd bring anything, even Medusa's head. (I feel like he didn't mean it and it was just an expression for "id get you literally anything. (but ofcourse not medusa's head)") Polydectes, who very much wanted Perseus gone, said "great, Medusa's head it is."

Sad Perseus... But Perseus had a secret weapon. Guess what it was. No, not courage.



It was nepotism. (Remember, his daddy is Zeus.)

### The game is on

Perseus asked the gods for help and they showed up:

- **Athena** gave him a polished, mirror-like shield so he could look at Medusa's reflection instead of her face
- **Hermes** gave him an unbreakable sickle made of adamant (basically the diamond-tier of Greek metals), a one-shot kill weapon
- **The nymphs** gave him flying sandals, a helm of invisibility (the Harry Potter one?) and a magic bag to keep Medusa's head in


After a journey I won't go deep into, he reached the cave where Medusa lived with her two sisters (yes, she had 2, even I only found out while reading). Medusa was the only mortal one of the three, which is why she was the target.

So our hero boy walks in moonwalking (remember, he can't look at her directly), watching her through the shield, and cuts her head off. She didn't see it coming, ig. He bags the head, but as Medusa's blood hits the ground, it gives birth to Pegasus, the winged horse.

> And if you know Pegasus from the Beyblade Metal series, yes, same one, Ginga's blade. Damn, these Greeks have their references everywhere.

Her sisters wake up and chase him, but our boy puts on the invisibility helm and flies away with the head, using the sandals (Pegasus did his own thing).

## Side Quest: A wife, because why not

On the way back he also picked up a wife (it's in Zeus's blood, I guess) named Andromeda.

> He rescued her from a sea monster, but that is a story in itself, so maybe another time. Told you, everywhere.

## Back at the palace

Back to the main story. Perseus returns to Seriphos, where Polydectes is still pressuring Danae to marry him. He is shocked to see Perseus alive and starts accusing him of not delivering his gift.

So Perseus asks his friends to look away (yes, even dead you can't look at that Gorgon), pulls the head out of the bag, and the king and all his men turn to stone in their shocked expressions.

And they all lived happily ever after.

-- The End --

## Wait, what about the prophecy?

Oh right, I forgot. So some years later, Acrisius heard Perseus was coming and fled to the city of Larissa. Perseus, who had no idea, showed up there to compete in a big athletic games. During the discus throw, the disc veered off (some versions say the wind carried it, others say Perseus just threw it too hard, I mean, he's a demigod, he could have kept it in check) and hit a man in the audience. You guessed it. That was Acrisius, his grandfather. The prophecy came true, and the man who tried so hard to avoid it caused it himself.

(Yeah, this part is short, but the real highlight was killing Medusa, so it makes sense, doesn't it?)

And finally, ----- The End -----

P.S. The constellations of Perseus, Andromeda and Pegasus are all right next to each other in the sky.

> Bonus: the star Algol in Perseus is traditionally said to be Medusa's eye, and it actually blinks. It dims every ~3 days because it's two stars eclipsing each other. Winking Gorgon, basically.
